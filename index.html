About and motivation

I came to computer science on purpose, not by default. I studied pre-medical at college, spent a few months working with technology before university, and found that building things suited me. I have not doubted the choice since.

Two events shaped what I care about. In 2022 monsoon floods devastated large parts of my country, and cloud hid the ground from satellites exactly when relief teams needed to see it. That became my final-year project. In 2025 a student at my university died, and I started asking why so few students use the help that exists. That became a study of its own.

Both taught me the same lesson. The algorithm is the smaller part of the work. What separates a demo from a tool is careful evaluation, honest reporting of where it fails, and a person who stays in charge of the decision.

A model is only as good as the evaluation around it.

I can build and ship complete systems. What I want now is depth: to understand the methods behind them well enough to design them, not only to apply them.

Final-year research project · Team of four · 2026

Sentinel Flux Core

Mapping floods from satellite radar

During Pakistan’s monsoon floods, cloud hides the ground from ordinary satellites. Radar sees through cloud. Our toolkit takes Sentinel-1 radar images from before and after a flood, maps the flooded area, and reports what the water is likely to reach.

The research question: can the map be trusted on a flood the model was never shown? We tested it only on whole flood events held out of training, so the score reflects new floods and not ones it has already seen.

My role Team coordinator and documentation lead. I wrote the report and designed the command-line tool that runs every stage.

Method, results and limits

Method

  • Open water looks dark to radar, so a flood shows up as land that went dark between two passes.
  • A classifier then refines the map using signal strength, change over time and terrain.
  • A person reviews every map in a dashboard before any message is approved.

Limits

  • One audit event scores 0.674. Finding out why is the next piece of work.
  • It maps floods that have happened. It does not predict them.
  • It supports a human decision. It is not an automatic warning system.

Self-initiated study · 2026 · Design stage

Digital Well-being

A private mental-health check-in for students

After a student died in December 2025, my university set up a well-being society where students can talk to teachers and a psychiatrist. Almost nobody goes. Walking into that office is a public act, and for many students that is the hardest part.

The research question: can the first step be taken in private? I led the design of a check-in that a student opens on their own phone. It asks questions built on two standard screening questionnaires, then suggests self-help or recommends a visit. It screens and never diagnoses.

37
questions, built on the PHQ-9 and GAD-7 questionnaires
84%
test accuracy, roughly, on a public student dataset
0
student records collected, until ethics approval
Safeguards and what is not done yet

Safeguards

  • Any sign of suicidal thoughts skips the model and shows crisis help at once.
  • No student data is collected before ethics approval.
  • A pilot would run under the society’s counsellors.

Not done yet

  • The app is not built.
  • The 84% figure comes from a public dataset. It says nothing yet about students at my university.
