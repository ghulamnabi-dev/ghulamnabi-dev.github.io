About and motivation

I came to computer science on purpose, not by default. I studied pre-medical at college, spent a few months working with technology before university, and found that building things suited me. I have not doubted the choice since.

Security is where my interest has settled. At work, part of my job is tracking and fixing vulnerable dependencies in a system that more than 100 people use. In my own projects I built sign-in, sessions and access control without a framework, so that I would understand what a framework hides. An eight-week internship then showed me the same software from the attacker’s side.

Cryptography is the part I most want to study properly. So far I have only used it: password hashing, signed tokens, random values compared in constant time. I know how to use these correctly. I do not yet know why they are secure, and that is what I want to learn.

Two other projects taught me about trust in a wider sense. One maps floods from satellite radar, and one is a mental-health check-in for students at my university. In both, the algorithm was the smaller part of the work. What mattered was careful testing, honest reporting of where it fails, and a person who stays in charge of the decision.

A system is only as trustworthy as the attempts made to break it.

I can build and ship complete systems. What I want now is depth: to understand the methods behind them well enough to design them, not only to apply them.

Security work

What I have done in security so far, and where each claim can be checked.

A web shop secured without a framework

For TechZone I wrote sign-in, sessions, request-forgery protection and access control myself, to understand them. I used only standard, well-tested building blocks. Nothing cryptographic is invented.

  • Passwords are hashed with bcrypt, and upgraded at sign-in when the settings change.
  • A failed sign-in takes the same time and gives the same message whether or not the account exists, so an attacker cannot learn which e-mails are registered.
  • Each session gets one random 256-bit anti-forgery token, compared in constant time.
  • The session ID is renewed at every sign-in, uploads are checked by their content, and every query uses prepared statements.

Checked on every push A forged cross-site request is rejected, the API never returns a password field, a failed sign-in gives the same message for any account, and 12 simultaneous checkouts for 3 units produce exactly 3 orders.

Access control, tested the way an attacker would

In PhoneBook the tests sign in as two different people and try to read each other’s data. Another account’s record returns “not found”, never “forbidden”, so the system does not even confirm that the record exists.

  • Sign-in tokens are signed with HMAC-SHA256. The server accepts that algorithm only, and checks the issuer, the audience and the expiry.
  • The server refuses to start if the signing secret is missing or too short.
  • In Mohalla a viewer who tries to change the accounts is refused by the server, not only by a hidden button.

Sentinel Flux Core

Mapping floods from satellite radar

During Pakistan’s monsoon floods, cloud hides the ground from ordinary satellites. Radar sees through cloud. Our toolkit takes Sentinel-1 radar images from before and after a flood, maps the flooded area, and reports what the water is likely to reach.

The research question: can the map be trusted on a flood the model was never shown? We tested it only on whole flood events held out of training, so the score reflects new floods and not ones it has already seen.

0.874

My role Team coordinator and documentation lead. I wrote the requirements, design, method and evaluation chapters of the report, and designed the command-line tool that runs every stage. The detection and classification code is the team’s work.

Method, results and limits

Method

  • Open water looks dark to radar, so a flood shows up as land that went dark between two passes.
  • A classifier then refines the map using signal strength, change over time and terrain.
  • A person reviews every map in a dashboard before any message is approved.

  • One audit event scores 0.674. Finding out why is the next piece of work.
  • It maps floods that have happened. It does not predict them.
  • It supports a human decision. It is not an automatic warning system.

Self-initiated study · 2026 · Design stage

Digital Well-being

A private mental-health check-in for students

After two students died within a single month in late 2025, my university set up a well-being society where students can talk to teachers and a psychiatrist. Almost nobody goes. Walking into that office is a public act, and for many students that is the hardest part.

The research question: can the first step be taken in private? I led the design of a check-in that a student opens on their own phone. It asks questions built on two standard screening questionnaires, then suggests self-help or recommends a visit. It screens and never diagnoses.

Safeguards and what is not done yet

Safeguards

  • Any sign of suicidal thoughts skips the model and shows crisis help at once.
  • No student data is collected before ethics approval.
  • A pilot would run under the society’s counsellors.

Not done yet

  • The app is not built.
  • The 84% figure comes from a public dataset. It says nothing yet about students at my university.

Projects

Four systems I built outside coursework, three of them complete. Each had a question it must not get wrong.

  • TechZone A PC-parts marketplace

    Can one seller read another shop’s order?

    No. Every query is limited to the seller’s own products and their own lines of a shared order, and another shop’s order returns “not found”. Checkout also survives a rush: on every push, a test sends 12 checkouts at once for 3 units and requires exactly 3 orders.

    Case studyCodePHP, MariaDB, JavaScript

  • PhoneBook A contacts system with accounts

    Can one account find out that another account’s contact exists?

    No. Asking for someone else’s contact returns “not found”, and tests sign in as two people to prove it. Imports are all or nothing, so a bad file never leaves half its contents behind.

    Case studyCodeC#, ASP.NET Core, Angular

  • Mohalla Finances for a housing society

    Who is allowed to change the accounts?

    Only admins, and the server decides, not the screen. A viewer’s attempt to write is refused. The monthly ledger is generated, so nobody types a balance.
